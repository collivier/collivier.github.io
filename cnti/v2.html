CNTi v2.0.0 and newer

“every failure tells you how to fix it, every result is trustworthy, and every command speaks GNU.”

What’s new in v2.0.0

  • cnf-testsuite renamed to cnti-testsuite and refocused on CNF essential and workload suites
  • Failures now come with fixes and remediation
  • A modern, scriptable CLI: GNU-style --options, kebab-case arguments, bash/zsh completion, classified exit codes
  • No more vacuous passes: static checks replace disabled tests, skipped tests report N/A

What’s new in v2.0.0 (2/2)

  • Previously disabled tests are back (CNI compatibility, α-K8s APIs, tracing)
  • Major security-toolchain upgrades (Kyverno, Kubescape, LitmusChaos, Crystal, Helm)
  • Leaner, faster, tidier runs: one cnti/ workspace, nothing unnecessary installed
  • Pipeline-friendly by design: results on stdout, logs on stderr

Full v2.0.0 changelog

free5GC and OCUDU validations

  • Added OCUDU as a second reference CNF after free5GC (see LF Networking blog and free5GC blog); both are validated nightly
  • Leveraged AI to triage results and code, classifying every failure as a suite bug or a genuine CNF characteristic
  • The bugs found in the CNTi workload suite during the CI/CD runs are fixed in v2.1.0

The CNTi workload suite is trustworthy

New CNTi badges

lfn-cnti/testsuite-action

  • installs the suite, creates a 3-node kind cluster and deploys your CNF
  • runs the essential tests
  • writes a job summary, annotates failed tests, uploads results as an artifact
  • fails the job when the objective is not met
  • shows a new CNTi badge CNTi essential 16/19 (passed / maximum), green when the objective is met

Simple, isn’t it?

permissions:
  contents: write
# ...
- uses: lfn-cnti/testsuite-action@v1
  with:
    helm_chart_dir: charts/my-cnf
    badge_branch: badges

Recommendations

  • Switch to the latest CNTi release — v2.2.0 today, v2.3.0 soon — to benefit from our hardening
